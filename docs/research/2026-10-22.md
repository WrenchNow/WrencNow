## Top 5 findings

1. **The main incumbent in Denver is a single merged company, and its local reviews are mixed.** Wrench/YourMechanic covers Denver under both brands. A Birdeye profile for "Wrench Mobile Mechanic – Denver, CO" shows about 1.7 stars on a very small, inconsistent review count ([Birdeye](https://birdeye.com/wrench-mobile-mechanic-denver-co-170008913975788)). Meanwhile, YourMechanic's own Denver make pages show 4.6–5.0 averages ([YourMechanic Denver](https://yourmechanic.com/city/denver-co)). Its Trustpilot profile is now titled "Wrench/YourMechanic" with roughly 17,000 reviews ([Trustpilot](https://pt.trustpilot.com/review/yourmechanic.com)).

2. **Customer complaints focus on price surprises and follow-through, not on the mobile model itself.** Recurring themes in reviews:
   - Final bills higher than the quote (one example: a $160 quote that became about $213).
   - Labor billed per part, so it exceeded the time actually worked.
   - Confusion over how the diagnostic credit works.
   - A part that was never installed, followed by a poorly handled return visit.

   Sources: [Trustpilot](https://dk.trustpilot.com/review/yourmechanic.com?page=2), [ComplaintsBoard](https://www.complaintsboard.com/yourmechanic-part-not-installed-c2012120). These are individual reviews, not audited data. Historically, YourMechanic quoted a fixed price per job and took a variable cut that depended on the job and the mechanic ([Vator, 2016](https://vator.tv/2016-07-01-how-does-yourmechanic-make-money/)).

3. **Colorado's Motor Vehicle Repair Act maps directly onto the product.**
   - Work requires the customer's consent and an estimate, given in writing or orally and recorded on the work order. The only exception is a vehicle that was towed in or left after hours, and then only up to $100 ([C.R.S. 42-9-104](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_42-9-104), [Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-42/motor-vehicle-repairs/article-9/section-42-9-104/)).
   - The work order must tell the customer they can have their replaced parts returned (§104(2)(a)(III), [FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/co/title-42-vehicles-and-traffic/co-rev-st-sect-42-9-104/)).
   - Going over the estimate by more than the lesser of 10% or $25 requires new consent (§106(1), [FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/co/title-42-vehicles-and-traffic/co-rev-st-sect-42-9-106/), [Colorado Lemon Law](https://www.coloradolemon.com/cmvra)).
   - Failing to provide a required estimate is a petty offense, and violations also count as deceptive trade practices under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act ([C.R.S. 42-9-112](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_42-9-112)).

   Whether WrenchNow or the mechanic counts as the "repair facility" is a question for counsel. I read the 10%/$25 figure from snippets only *(verify on leg.colorado.gov)*.

4. **Denver mechanic pay sets the bar for recruiting.**
   - A third-party site citing BLS May 2025 data puts the Denver-Aurora-Centennial median at about $29.79/hr (~$61,960/yr), across ~6,340 jobs ([WageDex](https://wagedex.com/jobs/automotive-service-technicians-and-mechanics/metro/denver-aurora-centennial-co)). *(verify on bls.gov; it didn't load this run.)*
   - Crowd-sourced Payscale data runs lower, with a median of about $24.78/hr ([Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/research/US/Job=Automotive_Service_Technician_%2F_Mechanic/Hourly_Rate/08f5b69d/Denver-CO)).
   - YourMechanic recruits independent contractors with ads saying "up to $1,500–$2,000/week." It requires 5+ years of experience, the mechanic's own tools and a clean background, and one LA listing cites $40–$50/hr flat rate ([YourMechanic job post](https://yourmechanic.theresumator.com/apply/9QpL5Q/EARN-UP-TO-2000WEEK-FIX-CARS-AS-A-MOBILE-MECHANIC), [Lerer Hippeau jobs](https://jobs.lererhippeau.com/companies/yourmechanic/jobs/51018858-earn-up-to-1-500-week-fix-cars-as-a-mobile-mechanic)).
   - Local pipeline: Emily Griffith Technical College (part of Denver Public Schools) runs an ASE-accredited automotive program ([Emily Griffith](https://www.emilygriffith.edu/about-us/)), with full-time and evening options ([program directory](https://skillplum.com/school/emily-griffith-technical-college-denver-co/470604-cert-1-2yr)).

5. **Demand tailwinds are real but not Denver-specific yet.**
   - The average US vehicle age reached 12.8 years in 2025 ([S&P Global Mobility](https://aftermarketinsight.spglobal.com/main/news/proc/create-pdf?id=4375)).
   - Some Colorado congressional districts average 13.3–15.5 years ([Alliance for Automotive Innovation](https://www.autosinnovate.org/resources/insights/co/3)).
   - Maintenance and repair prices were up 5.4% year over year as of Dec 2025 ([Stacker](https://stacker.com/stories/colorado/see-how-colorado-ranks-most-auto-repair-shops)).

   On AI, the 2026 launches are shop-side estimating tools, such as AutoTechIQ's AutoQuoteIQ inside Tekmetric (Mar 2026, [MOTOR](https://www.motor.com/2026/03/autotechiq-launches-autoquoteiq-for-faster-auto-estimating/)), plus consumer OBD/photo "fair price" apps ([AutoDiag AI](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6761052699)). I found no AI feature shipped by Wrench/YourMechanic or AutoNation Mobile Service.

## 3 recommendations

1. **Build the Colorado estimate rules into the booking flow and make them a selling point.** Every job gets a written itemized estimate, with parts listed as OEM, aftermarket, used or rebuilt (per the 42-9-102 definition of an estimate). Customer consent is timestamped. Any change above the 10%/$25 threshold needs re-approval in the app before work continues, and the customer can tick a box to get replaced parts back. This answers the #1 complaint about the incumbent. Site copy can say "you approve every change before it's charged" without quoting any research numbers. *(Owner: backend + legal review.)*

2. **Recruit from the Denver trade pipeline, with honest pay messaging.** Reach out to Emily Griffith's automotive program and its evening students. Pitch mechanics on the 95% take-home after the 5% platform fee rather than on vague "up to $X/week" claims. Keep the `basic` tier as an entry point for newer graduates, with `certified` unlocked by ASE credentials. Don't publish wage comparisons on the site.

3. **Get a legal and insurance read before launch.** Two questions: does WrenchNow or the mechanic count as the "repair facility" under the MVRA, and which policies must mechanics carry before going live (garage liability and commercial auto are the commonly cited ones; [Identifix guide](https://www.identifix.com/blogs/how-to-start-an-automotive-mobile-mechanic-business-11-steps/))? If proof of insurance is needed, add it as a go-live requirement next to the background check and Stripe onboarding. I couldn't verify Colorado or Denver licensing and sales-tax rules this run.
