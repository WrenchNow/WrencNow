## Research highlights
- Wrench and YourMechanic are one company (merged 2022) and the main Denver competitor. The top complaint is bills
  that come in over the quote. Our hard cap at the approved estimate answers that directly.
- Colorado's repair law requires written estimates and consent. The research says going over the estimate by more
  than the lesser of 10% or $25 needs re-approval, and that customers can ask for replaced parts back. Both figures
  need checking against the statute.
- Recruiting lead: Emily Griffith Technical College in Denver, an ASE-accredited program.

## Needs your decision
1. **Colorado estimate rules:** should we add timestamped written-estimate consent and a "return my old parts" option
   to booking? Recommended. It's a small change, but get a lawyer's read first on whether WrenchNow or the mechanic
   is the "repair facility" under the law.
2. **Proof of insurance:** should it become a go-live requirement for mechanics, alongside the background check
   and Stripe?
3. **No-show reporting:** customers can currently report a no-show 30 minutes after start, even if the mechanic
   marked "on the way" but didn't tap "start". Should mechanics be able to contest it, or should that state go to
   admin review?
